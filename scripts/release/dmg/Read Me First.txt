Snaglet for macOS — please read this first
=====================================

Three things to know before you start. They are quick, and none of
them means anything is wrong with the download.


1. INSTALL Snaglet — drag it to Applications
---------------------------------------

In the window that opened, drag the Snaglet icon onto the Applications
folder next to it. Then eject "Snaglet" from the sidebar of a Finder
window, and open Snaglet from your Applications folder.

Please don't run Snaglet straight from this disk image. macOS runs apps
opened from a disk image out of a temporary, read-only copy, so Snaglet
would stop working the moment you eject it. Snaglet detects this and
will tell you to move it, rather than failing in a confusing way.


2. FIRST LAUNCH — macOS will block Snaglet once
-------------------------------------------

The first time you open Snaglet, macOS refuses and warns that it cannot
verify the developer. This is expected. Snaglet is not yet notarized by
Apple, and macOS blocks the first launch of every app that isn't.
You only have to do this once.

  a. Open your Applications folder and double-click Snaglet.
     macOS shows a warning and refuses to open it. Click Done.

  b. Open System Settings, then go to Privacy & Security.

  c. Scroll down to the Security section. There is a message saying
     "Snaglet" was blocked to protect your Mac.

  d. Click "Open Anyway", then confirm with Touch ID or your password.

  e. Snaglet opens, and you will not be asked again.

On macOS 15 (Sequoia) and later — including macOS 26 — Control-clicking
the app and choosing "Open" no longer works as a shortcut for this.
Use System Settings as described above.

If you would rather use the Terminal, this single command does the
same thing:

    xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Snaglet.app


3. WHAT HAPPENS ON FIRST RUN
-----------------------------

Snaglet keeps everything on your own Mac, in a folder called ".snaglet" in your
home directory: its database, its logs, and your settings. There is no
Snaglet account, no Snaglet server and no telemetry. What you send to agents goes
to the model providers you connect. The very first launch takes a
little longer than usual because Snaglet sets up its local PostgreSQL
database, and it downloads its search model (about 137 MB) in the
background.
